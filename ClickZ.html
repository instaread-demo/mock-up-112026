Social Content Is Now a Discovery Asset: Ad Platforms and AI Tools Surface Brands From It

Many brands built discovery around their own websites. A shopper typed a query into a search engine and a ranking earned the click. The site then supplied the product details, reviews and checkout.

Shoppers now also meet brands through posts that creators, employees and communities publish. Platform search and AI tools surface those posts, which makes social content an important discovery asset.

AI tools and platform search read what others publish

Public posts are text that platform search and AI tools can match to a shopper’s question. Some brands are already planning around it.

Samantha Critchell, VP of corporate communications at e.l.f. Beauty, explained the company’s approach at The Lead Summit. For Critchell, published stories feed what AI systems learn from. So e.l.f. Beauty works with the outlets whose coverage those models draw on. “You say you’re training your models, we’re training our models for sure,” she said.

ClickZ reported that she also pointed to YouTube, LinkedIn and Reddit as other places that shape what AI tools return.

The brief decides what a post gets found for

If platforms and AI tools read a post’s text, the brand’s own brief is where that text first takes shape.

The brief should start from customer language. Reviews, comments and support questions show how people describe a product, and those phrases belong in it. For a stand mixer, a brief might name “stand mixer for bread dough” and ask for it in the caption and the opening line. The same request works for a video, an article or a thread, and brands can leave tone and delivery to the creator.

Repeat the phrase across the campaign, so one search leads to several posts instead of one.

Employees and creators extend a brand’s reach

The brief covers outside creators, and the same thinking helps inside the company. Subject experts already hold the product knowledge. A head of product who explains the product in a LinkedIn article gives AI tools a source they can cite. The article can carry the same customer phrasing the brief used.

One recent study points the same way. Goodie, an AI visibility firm, found that most cited LinkedIn URLs in AI answers sat outside company pages, mostly employees’ own articles. Creators and community members can fill the same role from outside the company, so they belong in the same plan.

Where discovery is heading

The next place a shopper meets a brand may be a sentence someone else wrote. That makes creator, employee and community content one discovery plan, with shared phrases and shared briefs.

Start with the language. Collect the phrases customers use in reviews and support questions. Share that list with the creator team, the employees who publish and the people who pitch press. Then run an audit. Search your five main product phrases on TikTok, Instagram and Reddit. Repeat in an AI tool such as ChatGPT or Perplexity and note which content appears.

Platforms are building for this. TikTok’s Search Hubs are brand-owned destinations at the top of its search results, and Branded Buzz connects creator videos to them. Our piece on how brands are rethinking influencer partnerships covers the creator side.

As platforms add brand tools to search, a brand’s briefed posts may reach shoppers before its own site does.
