Musical Monday: Hoosier Holiday (1943)

It’s no secret that the Hollywood Comet loves musicals.
In 2010, I revealed I had seen 400 movie musicals over the course of eight years. Now that number is over 800. To celebrate and share this musical love, here is my weekly feature about musicals.

This week’s musical:
Hoosier Holiday (1943) – Musical #862

Studio:
Republic Pictures

Director:
Frank McDonald

Starring:
Dale Evans, George Byron, Emma Dunn, Thurston Hall, Isabel Randolph, George D. Hay, Lillian Randolph, Shug Fisher, Nick Stewart, Ferris Taylor, Georgia Davis, Sleepy Williams, Gwen Verdon (uncredited), Jane Allan (uncredited), Nancy Brinckman (uncredited)
Themselves:
The Hoosier Hot Shots (Ken Trietsch, Paul Trietsch, Charles Ward, Frank Kettering)
The Music Maids (Patt Hyatt, Alice Ludes, Denny Wilson, Jeanne Darrell)
Three Shades of Rhythm (Louise Harris, Alyce Haynes Marcus, Lucius Smith)

Plot:
The Baker Brothers (Byron, Hoosier Hot Shots) are eager to enlist in the Army Air Force. However, the head of the local draft board, Henry P. Fairchild (Hall), refuses the brothers, because they are farmers, and their work is needed for the war effort. Mr. Fairchild also holds a personal grudge against the Baker family. The Baker brothers begin to woo the Fairchild sisters (Evans, Music Maids) so that their father will want to draft the boys to get them away from his daughters.

Notable Songs:
• “Hoosier Holiday” performed by Dale Evans and the chorus
• “Bow-Legged Cowboy” performed by The Hoosier Hot Shots
• “Boogie Woogie Hoe-Down” performed by Dale Evans and the Music Maids
• “The K. P. Serenade” performed by The Hoosier Hot Shots

My review:
During World War II, entertainment was used as a form of encouraging audiences into action. For example, BUCK PRIVATES promoted the early draft, TENDER COMRADE promoted women in war work and supporting each other while their loved ones were away, or YOUTH ON PARADE (1942) encouraged college students to stay in school, as knowledge is an important weapon.

This week’s Musical Monday has a unique message that I personally haven’t run across previously: Farmers serve an important role in the war off the battlefield.

In HOOSIER HOLIDAY (1943), the Baker Brothers (Byron, Hoosier Hot Shots) are eager to enlist in the Army Air Force. However, the head of the local draft board, Henry P. Fairchild (Hall), refuses the brothers, because they are farmers, and their work is needed for the war effort. Mr. Fairchild also holds a personal grudge against the Baker family. The Baker brothers begin to woo the Fairchild sisters (Evans, Music Maids) so that their father will want to draft the boys to get them away from his daughters.

I won’t spoil the ending, but it hits home that farmers are just as important to win the war, and they aren’t less for not carrying a gun and wearing a uniform.

Despite HOOSIER HOLIDAY being a lower budget, this movie was delightful. I loved the unique angle, and I also loved the brothers and sisters plotline. The actors in this film are unique because outside of Dale Evans and George Byron, the sisters and brothers are made up of musical groups. While they’re singing is great, their acting is also great. I wasn’t familiar with George Byron, so I actually assumed he was solely a singer. It’s fun to see Dale Evans in her pre-western and Roy Rogers films too (though I love Roy Rogers). In this, she’s simply a modern singing and dancing character.

The background is also great, including sweet Emma Dunn, blustering Thurston Hall, and humorous Lillian Randolph.

I really enjoyed all of the toe-tapping tunes in this film. But more than anything, I really enjoyed the unique farming angle of this story.

Admittedly, I had to purchase a questionable version of this film, which is unfortunate, but thankfully here, I could see and hear the film well. HOOSIER HOLIDAY (1943) runs at a brisk 65-minutes and every second is a great time.
