The ultimate guide to Manchester Christmas Markets

Jo Milligan has visited every stall at Manchester Christmas Markets. This is the honest truth about the best places to go for gifts and guzzling in 2026

Published 12 November, 2026
By Jo Milligan Editor
Share

Manchester Christmas Markets are huge – in terms of both size and popularity.

This year, there are 210 stalls spread out in a range of locations across the city centre. I have visited every single one of them and the wooden soles of my clogs are worn down to splinters to prove it.

The Christmas markets have a reputation for festive good times but also for tat and rip-off merchants.

This is your ultimate guide to the best places for gift shopping, eating and drinking so you can enjoy Manchester Christmas Markets in all their glory.

I’ve picked out my favourites in every location and tried to give you a sense of what the different settings offer.

Albert Square

The atmosphere

This is The Big One. The one everyone imagines when they think of Manchester Christmas Markets. Especially now Albert Square is open again. It has the best festive feeling of all this year’s locations but it’s also the busiest. It has the twin family attractions of the helter-skelter and the big wheel as well as photobooths, a lit-up walk-through tunnel for family photos and a selfie sleigh photo op too.

The stalls

Food traders include: Hot Chestnuts; The Flat Baker; Bratwurst King; The Wine Barrel Bar; The Fa La La Bar; Grilled Cheese Toastie stall; Toasted Marshmallows; North Pole Pub; Yorkshire Pudding Wrap stall; Mr Crimble Crumbles; Off-Piste Bar; Pancake House; Float Bar; Pat’s Poffertjes; Eat Greek; Just Get Loaded hash browns; French Toast.

Gift traders include: Popsters coasters; The Manchester Shop; Felt Friends; Cozy by Luciano selling cuddly toys; Manimu pearl jewellery; Amber with more jewellery; Old Maps; Dionysus Art with music pictures; Tatova Hata with Ukrainian baubles; From The Olive Tree featuring hand-carved nativity scenes from Bethlehem.

Our favourites for food and drink

Albert Square has that Christmas Market feeling in abundance and North Pole Pub edges it for festive drinking. Despite outdoor drinking being cold rather than cosy, North Pole Pub gives off traditional pub vibes with its welcoming glow. There are as many Christmas trees as Narnia and a sheltered drinking section that aims to bring at least some of the comforts of indoor drinking.

Food-wise it has to be The Flat Baker. This Ancoats bakery and patisserie is taking it to the next level with their first ever live bakery at the Christmas Markets. Order your favourite pastries fresh from the ovens or marvel at the piping and decoration happening right there in a cold market square. I can thoroughly recommend their chocolate orange pastries for a taste of Christmas.

Our favourites for gifts

Old Maps prints reproduction antique maps on linen. It gets bonus points for being the only cartography stall at the Christmas Markets. It’s not cheap with prices ranging from £28 – £95 depending on size but who doesn’t love a map? From souvenirs of countries you’ve visited to celebrating your heritage with maps of the counties of the UK and Ireland, a map makes a great gift. Or stay closer to home with maps of Manchester before the skyscrapers came to town. Frames not included.

Lincoln Square

The atmosphere

This is a small food and drink hideaway that’s new for this year – a respite from the crowds of Albert Square. It also feels very much like an afterthought.

The stalls

Food traders include: Old Saint Nick’s Christmas Bar; Chen’s Happy House with salt and pepper chicken; Falafel King; Loaded Gyro; Deck The Buns with smash burgers; Fuego 1987; Steak Out; Dine With Saira; Wok + Whisk; Christmas Churros; BangGa BangGa.

Our favourites for food and drink

If you’re carousing in Lincoln Square you’ll have to bring the atmosphere yourself. Old Saint Nick’s Christmas Bar is a good choice for some quieter drinks and it has quite a cosy feel, the truck giving it a bit of personality. It doesn’t have a huge range of craft beer considering that’s supposed to be its USP but if Goose Island IPA is good enough for the gander…

King Street

The atmosphere

There isn’t a lot of goodwill going on here. You get the sense that the shops are the main event and the stalls are just clogging up the street. That said, the very best traders bring their own atmosphere with bonhomie radiating and bringing in the crowds. There’s a bit of a foodie slant to the traders on this stretch so it could be a quick and easy place to pick up some gifts.

The stalls

Food traders include: Waffle Kart; The Greek; Ballaro Sicilian Street Food; The Great North Pie Co serving hot pies; Cheezy Bandits with mac’n’cheese; Hong Kong French Toast; Le Cuisine Provencale; Grumpy Moose Gin Bar; Float; Bratwurst King.

Gift traders include: The Best of Italy; Spirit of Manchester Distillery; Handmade Toffees + Fudges; Alyssa’s House with press-on nails; Salford Distillery; Chocolate Kisses; Great British Cheese Co; French Soap From Provence; Cannoli Hut; Personalised Tree Decorations.

Our favourites for food and drink

Float is a classier option than the usual gluhwein glugging places and is a good choice for cocktails or a bit of festive fizz. A good choice for a few drinks after work or after Christmas shopping but it doesn’t have the full Christmas Market feeling.

Our favourites for gifts

Perhaps we’re cheating with The Best of Italy because it’s also a food trader in a way but you don’t go to the Christmas Markets for a boozy hot chocolate and a nibble on a block of pecorino. This place smells as soon as you get near and I mean that in a good way. It’s real produce not endless truckles like you see at the other cheese stalls. They also sell legs of Parma ham which would make an imaginative gift.

Spirit of Manchester Distillery is another must-visit stall to get in some of the good stuff if you have people coming over at Christmas. Their Manchester Gin sets the standard in the city.

St Ann’s Square

The atmosphere

Apart from Albert Square, this is the location with the most atmosphere. It skews towards food and drink rather than shopping but it really looks the part.

The stalls

Food traders include: Original German & Hofbrau Bier Bar; Loaded Giant Yorkshire Puddings; Pancake House; Oh My Donut; Hot Chestnuts; Ma Cuisine Francaise; BangGa BangGa; Yard & Coop; Mr Crimble Crumbles; Guinness Bar; Cirque de Noel Bar; Loaded Roasties; Little Piece of Bahia; Gingerbread House; Niwakoku matcha.

Gift traders include: Amber selling jewellery; The Cheeseboard; Johnson Crafts; Pure Indulgence Soaps; Mon Ami with tea and cookware.

Our favourites for food and drink

For many of us, it just isn’t Christmas without a sausage and some mulled wine. If that’s what you fancy, The Original German & Hofbrau Bier Bar is the best option across Manchester Christmas Markets.

For a sweet treat, I can recommend the apple and cinnamon coin bbang from BangGa BangGa. This is their biggest outlet at the Christmas market so it does Korean desserts as well as corn dogs.

Our favourites for gifts

Mon Ami is one of our favourites at Manchester Christmas Markets. This fourth generation family business specialises in beautifully-crafted cast-iron teapots and healthy green tea to match.

Exchange Street

The atmosphere

This is really just an extension of St Ann’s Square without a separate vibe of its own.

The stalls

Food traders include: Hash & Dash fully-loaded hash browns; C&C with chocolate, unseasonal strawberries and coffee.

Gift traders include: Great British Cheese Co; Francoz Garden with miniature clay flowers; Su Mace Designs for artwork; Kin Vodka; Ridiculously Rich selling brownies as featured on The Apprentice; Pure with natural pet food as featured on Dragon’s Den.

Our favourites for food and drink

Just keep on walking to St Ann’s Square.

Our favourites for gifts

Kin Vodka was drawing in the afternoon crowds with their friendly stallholders and free samples. The bottles of craft vodka looked sleek and the toffee vodka liqueur was going down a storm. However, it was the chocolate rum liqueur that got my vote.

Market Street

The atmosphere

Market Street already has its own atmosphere if you could call it that and it’s not a selling point. However, suspend your disbelief – the Christmas markets on Market Street are actually an improvement. They add a bit of jollity to the grim consumerism but they do slow groups of shoppers down even further if that is possible. The vibe here is much more gift shopping than stuffing your face.

The stalls

Market Street is not the place to go for hot food or drinking. Traders include: Popsters coasters; Tatova Hata with Ukrainian baubles; Charlie Boy’s Barkery for dog treats; Loaded Bases for quirky Subbuteo artwork; Misu Tiramisu; WoodShyne for music art on wood; O’Donnell Moonshine; Momentum Jewellery; LFH Studio for cool prints; Socktopus; PC9 for knitted keyrings; Gabs Wonderland for press-on nails; Aurelie Welded Jewellery; Bulbzzle Jewellery; Storymaker Mead; Kolour Pop with anime and manga-inspired gifts; For The Hair You Wear with hair claws; Solid Cologne; Chocolate Kisses; Finest Fudge; Great British Cheese Co; Personalised Tree Decorations; Kings Jewelz and a few crusty stalls with joss sticks and Levellers fashion.

Our favourites for gifts

LFH is a great place for prints to brighten up your space. Think middle-aged basic wall art in the Live, Laugh, Love vein but importantly, not naff. These pictures are colourful, joyful and kitsch. And if you’re not having enough fun browsing pics of bouquets bursting out of bottles of ketchup, there’s a hands-on naughty-or-nice machine that delivers mantras like a Christmas market fortune cookie.

Also, the crackers from O’Donnell Moonshine contain more exciting surprises than miniature screwdrivers. Lastly, I’m not sure about Eyedentity. I can’t decide if a family picture of everyone’s irises is creepy or cool. It’s certainly unique, just like the eyes – the ocular equivalent of the human fingerprint.

New Cathedral Street

The atmosphere

New Cathedral Street is quite a classy selection of gift stalls with a hulking Witch House on the end – probably Manchester Christmas Market’s worst place for a festive drink. It seems somehow down-at-heel despite being open less than a week when I visited. The vibe is that of a flat roof estate pub’s outdoor smoking area.

The stalls

Food traders include: The Witch House; Fusion Korean BBQ; La Bavette serving steak frites; Java Hot Chocolate.

Gift traders include: Beauty Secrets with handmade soy wax candles; Diamontt Leather with unusual bound notebooks; The Cheshire Cheese Co; Eat Liquorice; Birchwood Leather; Carter & Belle with faux fur accessories; Where The Heart Is selling Manchester-themed gubbins; The Little Gift Hut with angel-related tat; Mr Murray’s Scotch Eggs; FairyFairs with Lithuanian ceramic candle houses.

Our favourites for food and drink

Head back to Exchange Square for hot food or pick up one of Mr Murray’s Scotch Eggs for an on-the-go snack. The smoked cheese & jalapeno were very good and I’m going back for a chorizo one.

Our favourites for gifts

One complaint that people have about the Christmas Markets is that everything is overpriced. Now I’m going to recommend something expensive. Sorry. Yes, you could go to B&M Bargains and pick up some homeware for a fraction of the price, but the Lithuanian candle houses at FairyFairs are super cute and they would make a lovely Christmas decoration. They’re not for everyone but they’ll bring back memories every time you light them. Am I becoming middle-aged? Maybe it’s all a bit Lilliput Lane Cottages but I do like them so there.

Exchange Square

The atmosphere

This is the most squished of all the Christmas market locations. You do feel a bit hemmed in. There are a few retail traders but it’s mainly big food and drink stalls.

The stalls

Food traders include: The Mill Exchange for mulled wine; Bratwurst King; Porky Pig; Fuego 1987; Mamma Mia for pizza; Dine with Saira; Holy Duck.

Gift traders include: Pure Indulgence Soaps; Ivy Rose Baby Boutique; Lucela’s Chocolate & Rum; From The Olive Tree featuring hand-carved nativity scenes from Bethlehem.

Our favourites for food and drink

The queues are all for Holy Duck with all things duck and duck fat with a bit of cheese thrown in to help the cholesterol as well as Fuego 1987 with its birria and tacos.

Corn Exchange

The atmosphere

Essentially a row of cute little Alpine huts in front of the Corn Exchange and largely indistinguishable from Exchange Square. However, this location is purely for gift shopping and food to take away whereas Exchange Square concentrates more on its hot food offering.

The stalls

Traders include: The Cheeseboard; The Crusty Pie Company; AK Distillery; The Duchess of Candles; Pebble & Grain pebble art; Make Up Or Break Up; Bibi Jewellery; Bakes by Millie; Simla beaded jewellery; Alpaca Wool Jumpers.

Our favourites for gifts

Alpaca wool jumpers look cosy but not itchy and they have a pleasing 1980s Greenwoods menswear vibe. Better than a Christmas jumper. Children might also like the fluffy mini alpacas.

Cathedral Gardens

The atmosphere

The big draw is Skate Manchester and there is a small collection of food traders to satisfy the skaters. There’s also a mini version of the big wheel to while away the minutes waiting for your ice rink slot.

The stalls

Food traders include: Spice Bags; Toasted Marshmallows; Pat’s Poffertjes; Off-Piste Bar; Fondue; Kingdom of Sweets; Warm Up At The Winter Bar.

Our favourites for food and drink

The Irish Spice Bags are my top recommendation and given how they’re having a bit of a moment I’m surprised there aren’t a few more of them around the markets. I like to keep it traditional but you can go festive if you want some cranberry gravy to make your chips soggy.

Manchester Christmas Markets FAQs

When are the Manchester Christmas Markets 2026 open?

Manchester Christmas Markets run from 6 November to 22 December 2026, with Albert Square and Cathedral Gardens staying open until 3 January 2027. Most gift stalls open 10am–9pm and food and drink stalls 11am–9pm, with shorter hours on certain dates. The markets are closed on Christmas Day.

Where are Manchester Christmas Markets located in 2026?

Manchester Christmas Markets are spread across ten walkable city centre locations: Albert Square, Lincoln Square, King Street, St Ann’s Square, Corn Exchange, Exchange Street, New Cathedral Street, Market Street, Exchange Square and Cathedral Gardens. Albert Square is the main hub, while Lincoln Square is a new food and drink-focused location for 2026.

What food and drink is available at Manchester Christmas Markets 2026?

Manchester Christmas Markets offer festive favourites including bratwurst, Yorkshire pudding wraps, mulled wine and hot chocolate. For something different, try Irish spice bags in Cathedral Gardens, tartiflette in St Ann’s Square, Korean corn dogs in Lincoln Square and steaming hot pies on King Street.

Are there vegetarian and vegan food options at Manchester Christmas Markets?

Yes, vegetarian and vegan options are available, although choices vary between stalls. Look out for dishes such as Vegan Hash from The Hash in Albert Square, and options like mac and cheese, pizza and curries. Check with individual traders about vegan ingredients and preparation methods, particularly where equipment is shared.

How do I get to Manchester Christmas Markets?

Manchester Christmas Markets are easily reached by public transport. Nearby Metrolink stops include St Peter’s Square, Exchange Square, Market Street and Victoria. Manchester Piccadilly, Victoria and Oxford Road railway stations are also within walking distance. Public transport is recommended during the busy festive period. For more information, see Transport for Greater Manchester’s Christmas Markets travel guide.

What family-friendly activities are there at Manchester Christmas Markets?

Beyond the food and drink stalls, Albert Square has a 50-metre Ferris wheel, a traditional helter-skelter and a giant Santa. Cathedral Gardens has a covered ice rink (open until 3rd January 2027), mini-fairground games and free live entertainment. Some attractions require paid tickets.
