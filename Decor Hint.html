This Old-Fashioned North Carolina General Store Is Hosting A Special Christmas Shopping Day With Santa And Free Treats

ByMira Ellsworth November 20, 2026November 19, 2026

Ho ho ho, forget the mall.

I’m heading straight for the North Carolina store where a 1922 cash register still rings up purchases like it never got the memo about modern shopping.

The place has been around since 1890, and even the bricks have a story because they were made from clay dug right on the property.

Advertisement

Now picture me showing up on one special Saturday while the old shop fills with hot chocolate, boiled peanuts, and visiting vendors. Perfect.

No online cart. No frantic clicking. No package tracking.

You actually have to walk through the door, browse the shelves, and talk to somebody. I approve.

The kids can find Santa while the grown-ups slow down for once.

Honestly, if my sleigh had a parking brake, I’d use it here.

Advertisement

Santa Picked A Saturday, And You Should Circle It Now

Mark Saturday, November 28, 2026, on your calendar right now. Davis General Merchandise is hosting its 4th Annual Small Business Saturday Event, and Santa is on the guest list.

Advertisement

The big guy arrives at 10:00 AM and stays until noon. That gives you a two-hour window, so plan your morning around it.

The whole event runs much longer, from 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM. That’s a long day of shopping inside one of the oldest family stores in North Carolina.

Here’s a friendly tip for parents. Get the Santa visit done first, then let everyone browse with full attention.

Kids tend to shop better after their wish list is safely delivered. Grown-ups tend to shop better after the kids stop asking when Santa gets there.

Small Business Saturday lands right after Thanksgiving every year. It’s meant to celebrate local shops instead of giant chain stores.

Few local shops fit that idea better than this one. The same family has run it since 1890, which is a pretty strong small business résumé.

So skip the mall parking lot this year. Santa found a better spot, and he’s clearly got good taste.

Advertisement

Hot Chocolate And Boiled Peanuts Make A Very Southern Combo

Hot chocolate in one hand and boiled peanuts in the other. That’s the kind of holiday snack plan only the South could dream up.

Advertisement

During the Small Business Saturday Event, visitors can enjoy hot chocolate, boiled peanuts, and samples from vendors while browsing the store.

Boiled peanuts are already a regular favorite at this shop. Plenty of folks stop in just for a bag, so getting them free feels like a bonus round.

The vendor samples add a fun twist to the day. Many products here come from independent businesses around the Carolinas.

That means a sample could lead you to a new jam, sauce, or snack you’d never find at a big grocery chain. Tasting first also makes gift shopping a lot easier.

Here’s a smart move for families. Let the kids sip hot chocolate while they wait for Santa, and the line will feel much shorter.

Treats like these work best when you don’t rush them. Take a cup, wander the aisles, and let the morning unfold slowly.

After all, free snacks taste even better in a building that has seen more than a century of holidays.

You’ll find the store at 8940 Old Statesville Rd, Charlotte, NC 28269. The Croft area sits between Charlotte and Huntersville, so it’s an easy drive for much of the region.

Advertisement

A 1922 Cash Register Still Rings Up Your Gifts

Most stores upgrade their checkout every few years. Davis General Merchandise still uses a cash register from 1922, and it works just fine.

Advertisement

That old machine is only one piece of the history here. The store also keeps an early Coca-Cola bottle opener and remnants of a wall-mounted box that once held the first telephone in the Croft community.

Look around and you’ll spot parts of a primitive fire hydrant too. There’s also a large wooden water bucket that once served as an early fire extinguisher.

Back then, fighting fires meant grabbing a bucket and moving fast. Today, it’s a great conversation starter for curious kids.

The original shelving is still visible throughout the store. So when you pull a gift off the shelf, you’re using fixtures with serious age on them.

Kids who love museums will have fun hunting for these old treasures. Turn it into a holiday scavenger hunt while you wait for Santa.

Ask them to find the bottle opener, the bucket, and the register. Whoever spots all three first could win the honor of picking the first candy.

History lessons rarely come with peanuts and Santa nearby. This one does, and that’s a winning deal.

Advertisement

The Brick Walls Came Straight From The Ground Below

Some buildings use bricks shipped from far away. This one used clay dug right from the property where it stands.

Advertisement

The two-story brick building was finished in 1908. It’s now recognized as a designated historic landmark, which makes your holiday shopping trip feel a little bit fancy.

The business itself is even older than the building. Brothers S.W. Davis and C.S. Davis started it in 1890 out of their family home.

The store first served farmers in the Croft community. Until the 1920s, every item arrived by rail, which explains why the tracks feel so close.

Trains still roll past today, and their horns add a fun soundtrack to the visit. You can even buy a train whistle inside if your kids need a new way to make noise.

Fair warning to parents. That whistle may get used all the way home.

Plan to snap a photo out front. Those homemade bricks deserve their moment.

Your Stocking Stuffer Problem Just Got Solved

Advertisement

Barrels of old-fashioned hard candy greet you near the front. Good luck walking past them without grabbing a bag.

Stocking stuffers are easy to find here. The store sells candy, Moon Pies, pocket knives, socks, and soap, all perfect for small surprises.

Glass bottle sodas make another fun pick. Options include Cheerwine, Blenheim Ginger Ale, and Uncle Scott’s Root Beer.

Cheerwine is a North Carolina classic, so it makes a great gift for anyone who moved away. A bottle in a stocking is a little taste of home.

Blenheim Ginger Ale has its own fans thanks to its famous spicy kick. You can even buy glass bottle sodas by the case if you’re stocking up.

Pocket knives can also make practical gifts for adults who appreciate useful everyday tools. Pair one with a candy bag, and you’ve got a stocking that feels thoughtful.

A quick budget idea: set a small limit per person and let everyone pick their own treasures. It keeps things fun and fair.

Honestly, the hardest part is choosing which candy flavor goes home with you.

Advertisement

Cast Iron Makes A Gift That Outlasts Every Gadget

Cast iron skillets can last for generations. That makes them a pretty fitting gift from a store that has lasted since 1890.

Advertisement

Davis General Merchandise carries a solid selection of cast iron cookware, including Lodge products. Skillets and pots are both on the shelves.

Cooks on your list will appreciate something sturdy and practical. A good pan gets used almost every day, unlike that gadget sitting in a drawer.

Workwear is another big draw here. The store carries Carhartt apparel, overalls, and plenty of boot options.

Overalls might sound like a funny gift at first. Then someone tries on a soft pair and suddenly wants to wear them everywhere.

Carhartt jackets and gear are great for people who work outside. They also fit anyone who just likes clothes that hold up.

Hardware shoppers won’t feel left out either. The store still stocks tools and supplies for everyday fixes around the house.

Shopping for a hard-to-please dad or grandpa? This aisle might be your answer.

And since it’s all in person, you can feel the weight of that skillet before you buy it.

Advertisement

Local Honey And Jams Turn Into Easy Hostess Gifts

Holiday parties call for a small gift at the door. A jar of local honey handles that job beautifully.

Advertisement

The store stocks local honey, jams, jellies, and preserves. Many products come from independently owned businesses across the Carolinas.

Buying here means your gift supports more than one family business. That’s a nice bonus during a season built around giving.

Handmade dish towels and baskets add even more options. Tuck a jar of jam into a basket with a towel, and you’ve built a gift set in minutes.

Muscadine juice is another fun find on the shelves. It’s made from a grape that grows well in the South.

Hostess gifts don’t need to be expensive to feel special. They just need to show a little thought.

A handwritten tag explaining where you bought it adds a personal touch. People love knowing their gift came from a store with this much history.

Try the vendor samples on event day before deciding. Tasting first means you can pick the flavor you’d actually want yourself.

Then maybe grab a second jar for your own kitchen. No one has to know.

Advertisement

TV Fans Might Recognize The Front Of This Building

Hollywood found this place before many locals did. The store appeared in the Cinemax series Banshee as a fictional business called The Forge.

Advertisement

That’s a fun bit of trivia to share while waiting in the Santa line. Most general stores can’t claim a starring role on TV.

Inside, there’s a small display dedicated to the show. Fans can check it out between browsing the candy and the cast iron.

The building’s look explains why filmmakers liked it. Old brick, original shelving, and real history are hard to fake on a set.

Still, the real star is the family behind the counter. Davis General Merchandise has stayed family-owned since it opened.

Silas Davis, grandson of founder S.W. Davis, ran the store until 2023.

His children Andrew Davis, Noelle Small, and Lois Ellen Tuttle now carry it forward.

That kind of family story is rare in North Carolina retail today. Most businesses change hands many times in a century.

So enjoy the TV connection. Just remember the store’s best role has always been serving its neighbors.

Advertisement

Bring Cash, Comfy Shoes, And A Little Patience

Cash comes in handy here. Prices are lower if you pay with cash, so stop by the ATM before you go.

Advertisement

The event runs from 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM, which is much longer than a normal Saturday. Regular Saturday hours end at noon, so this day is special.

On other weeks, the store opens weekdays at 9:30 AM, except Wednesday. It stays closed on Wednesday and Sunday.

Wear comfortable shoes because there’s a lot to explore. Garden supplies, feed and seed, toys, and clothing fill the space.

Gardeners will love that seeds are sold by the scoop. It’s an old-school touch that feels perfect in a building from 1908.

There’s no online store, so this trip is your chance to shop. If you spot something great, grab it before you leave.

Need a knife sharpened? The store has offered knife sharpening, which makes a handy extra errand.

Small Business Saturday in North Carolina rarely gets this charming. Santa, free treats, and over a century of history all under one roof?

That’s a holiday tradition worth starting this year.
