How Late Are Doctors Open in Australia?

ByFamily Doctor Editorial 25/07/2026
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 4:17
Instaread

Most Australian GP clinics close between 5pm and 6pm on weekdays, but many run evening sessions. Across the Family Doctor network, 11 of 105 clinics currently stay open until 7pm or later on at least one weekday, the latest closing at 8pm. Beyond clinic hours, after-hours home-visit services, Medicare Urgent Care Clinics and telehealth cover the gap. In an emergency, call 000.

Which GP clinics are open late?

Evening opening varies clinic by clinic. Live Family Doctor network data shows 11 of 105 active clinics with at least one weekday session running to 7pm or later — eight in Victoria, plus clinics in the ACT, Queensland and Western Australia — with the latest closing time currently 8pm. The GP clinics open late page lists every one of them with its evening hours and an online booking link, refreshed from the network’s live opening-hours data.

Can I see a GP after hours?

Yes — after-hours general practice exists in several forms. Evening sessions at regular clinics are the first option. After that, after-hours home-visit doctor services operate overnight and on weekends in most capital cities, usually booked from the evening onward. Government-funded Medicare Urgent Care Clinics take walk-ins for urgent but non-life-threatening problems into the evening. And the free healthdirect helpline (1800 022 222) can triage you to the right service at any hour. See our after-hours services page for how these fit together.

Can I see a doctor online at night?

Telehealth is the most accessible after-hours option for problems that do not need a physical examination. Many practices offer telehealth to existing patients during extended hours, and MyGPOnline — Family Doctor’s private online GP service — runs video and phone consultations after hours, including for medical certificates and general advice. As a private service, fixed fees apply and there is no Medicare rebate; pricing is shown before you book.

Are evening GP appointments bulk billed?

Billing after hours works the same way as during the day, but policies differ more between clinics. Some clinics bulk bill eligible patients — such as children and concession card holders — in evening sessions; others charge private fees with a Medicare rebate for eligible patients, and some apply different billing after certain times. Fees vary, so check the clinic’s page or call before booking. Our guide What is bulk billing? explains eligibility.

Why are so few clinics open late?

Evening sessions depend on having enough doctors to roster across extended hours, and general practice across Australia has faced sustained workforce shortage — so most practices concentrate their doctors in standard hours where demand is highest. That is why late opening clusters in larger clinics and metropolitan areas. It is also why the after-hours system is deliberately layered: evening clinic sessions first, then home-visit deputising services and urgent care clinics, then telehealth — rather than every clinic staying open late.

How do I find a clinic open late near me?

Start with the open-late list, which shows each clinic’s actual evening hours rather than a generic “open late” label — worth checking, because directories and search results often show stale hours. Confirm the hours on the clinic’s own page when you book, especially around public holidays. If nothing near you runs late enough, book the first appointment of the next morning or use one of the after-hours options above in the meantime.

Are doctors open on weekends?

Yes. Weekend opening is far more common than late-night opening: 47 of the 105 Family Doctor clinics open on Saturdays, and 14 also open on Sundays. The GP clinics open Saturday page lists them all by state with live hours and booking links.

When is a late-night problem an emergency?

Do not wait for a clinic to open — call 000 now — for chest pain or pressure, difficulty breathing, signs of stroke (facial droop, arm weakness, slurred speech), severe bleeding, sudden severe headache, seizures, or loss of consciousness. If you are unsure whether something can wait until morning, GP or emergency department? walks through the decision, and healthdirect (1800 022 222) can advise you live.
