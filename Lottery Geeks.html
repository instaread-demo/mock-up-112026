    If You Win Tuesday’s Mega Millions Jackpot, Take The Annuity!

    The grand prize sits at an estimated $600 million after 31 straight rollovers

    By Eric Raskin

    Last updated: November 17, 2026

    2 min

    Listen to audio version of this article

    It’s probably not Lottery Geeks’ place to tell anyone what to do with their money, but that won’t stop us from at least making a strong suggestion.

    The data available online indicates that under 5% of jackpot winners opt for the annuity payments, with more than 95% going the lump-sum route. We get it. Who knows where you’ll be in 30 years? May as well take the sure thing now.

    But if someone out there happens to win Tuesday’s Mega Millions jackpot, this is the time to go the annuity route. No, not because it’s better for the winner. Rather, because it’s better for the lottery business.

    After 31 consecutive rollovers since a ticket-buyer in Florida won on July 28, the estimated grand prize for Tuesday night’s drawing is $600 million. What a nice, big, bright, even, round number.

    The cash option? That’s $246.9 million. What an ugly, uneven, forgettable number.

    If someone wins and claims $600 million, that’s an eye-catching headline that helps promote Mega Millions. If someone wins and collects $246.9 million, that’s not much of a headline at all.

    And Mega Millions, which has been struggling ever since elevating its price point from $2 to $5 in the spring of 2025, could use some positive headlines.

    So, on the one hand, improving your life should be priority No. 1 if you smash that jackpot this week. But on the other hand … saying you won 600 million bucks would be pretty cool, right?

    With the jackpot at $555 million this past Friday night, the numbers came out 10, 22, 33, 49, 57, and gold Mega Ball 18, and nobody matched all six. One player in New York did connect on everything but the Mega Ball, however, and won $2 million before taxes.

    Lottery Geeks estimates 7.8 million tickets were sold for Friday’s drawing, climbing incrementally from 7.2 million the draw before that.

    Piddling Powerball

    Compared to Mega Millions, the Powerball jackpot isn’t all that exciting these days, having reset after a New Jersey customer won the second-biggest jackpot of the year on Nov. 4. For Wednesday’s drawing, it sits at a mere $92 million, with a cash option of $38.5 million.

    In Monday night’s drawing, with winning numbers 17, 35, 48, 60, 69, and red Powerball 17 (yes, double 17s were drawn), nobody hit the jackpot and nobody scored the Match 5. The largest prize won was $100,000, by two ticket buyers who each connected on four white balls and the red Powerball.

    Tickets for Mega Millions cost $5 and Powerball tickets cost $2. Most states offer the multiplier option for Powerball, which impacts any prize won except the jackpot, for an additional $1. Mega Millions comes with an automatic multiplier for any prize except the jackpot.

    Both Powerball and Mega Millions are legal in all states except Alabama, Alaska, Hawaii, Nevada, and Utah. Tickets for both draw games are also sold in Washington, D.C. and the U.S. Virgin Islands. In Puerto Rico and the U.K., customers can buy tickets for Powerball, but not for Mega Millions.
