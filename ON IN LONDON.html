Christmas at Handel Hendrix House

November 19, 2026 /
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 5:12
Instaread

Did you know two great musicians lived next door to each other in London? They weren’t separated by distance, but by time, around 200 years.

Today, their neighbouring homes at 23 and 25 Brook Street form Handel Hendrix House, one of London’s most unique museums. Tonight (19 November) marked the launch of its festive program, which runs until 3 January. The Georgian townhouse has been decked out for Christmas with beautifully decorated trees throughout and festive greenery winding its way up the bannisters.

The first sight of the main Christmas tree is genuinely jaw-dropping. Decorated with golden musical instruments, sheets of music and red feathers, it perfectly captures the spirit of a museum dedicated to two musical icons. As this was my third Christmas event of the season, and seeing the Christmas lights in the area on my walk to the station, it really feels like Christmas is just around the corner (35 days in fact). 

I had visited the museum several years ago but thoroughly enjoyed returning. Handel lived here for 36 years, from 1723 until his death in 1759, and it was within these walls that he composed some of his most celebrated works. Given that this includes Messiah, a Christmas visit feels especially appropriate. His restored bedroom, parlour, dining room and kitchen offer a glimpse into 18th-century life. I particularly enjoyed reading the information panels and listening to excerpts of his music. 

Walking up to the top floor, we can explore Hendrix’s restored flat, where he lived in the 1960s.  We see one of his guitars, explore displays of the records that inspired him and admire the colourful fashion of the 60s. The contrast between these 20th-century rooms and Handel’s 18th-century surroundings is what makes the museum so fascinating. As a Georgian townhouse, there are uneven floors and creaking staircases which remind us these were  lived-in homes. 

One of my favourite details from the museum is the connection between the two musicians. When Hendrix discovered that Handel had once lived next door, he reportedly went out and bought Handel’s music to listen to.

Before leaving, I couldn’t resist a browse in the gift shop which stocks gifts inspired by Handel, Hendrix and music more generally. I somehow managed to add three books to my ever-growing  to-be-read pile. I also picked up a children’s introduction to The Nutcracker for my two-year-old niece, who loves music, along with a gold sparkly musical Christmas ornament. As a former clarinetist, the treble clef decoration felt like the obvious choice.

Looking ahead, the Christmas programme offers plenty to enjoy, from live cooking demonstrations in Handel’s historic kitchen to festive concerts and seasonal performances.

As Christmas events go, Handel Hendrix House offers something refreshingly different. Alongside the festive decorations and seasonal atmosphere, visitors can explore the remarkable stories of two musical icons who called Brook Street home more than 200 years apart. It is a unique combination of history, music and Christmas cheer, making it a delightful way to spend a time London.

Events in December 

  • Hallelujah House: Inside Handel’s HQ – New exhibition offers fresh insights into the music that Handel composed and performed whilst calling 25 Brook Street home runs until 14 March 2027. Entry included in admission. 
  • Ronnie Wood: The Jimi Hendrix Portraits featuring two new, unseen original portraits of Jimi Hendrix in his flat on Brook Street by Ronnie Wood alongside prints of much-loved paintings inspired by photographs of Jimi.  On show until 11 April 2027. Entry included in admission. 
  • Hendrix in London – Step into Jimi Hendrix’s London and discover the story behind the music. Drawing on a remarkable archive of previously unseen material, the display offers a rare glimpse into Hendrix’s life, work and the world that surrounded him during his time at 23 Brook Street. Entry included in admission. 
  • Thursday 3 December –  Messiah 2026 at London Handel Festival
  • Saturday 5  December. Join resident guitarist Nigel Jones to learn the inside track on Hendrix’s legendary techniques and sounds. Bring your guitar along, or borrow one from the house, and jam in the place that Hendrix called home.   £22 including general admission
  • Sunday 6 December at 6pm- Handel Youth Ensemble: Rising Notes. Handel Youth Ensemble, which launched in September is an exciting new chamber ensemble exploring early music at baroque pitch for under 18s. Tickets include entry to Handel’s House (Tickets £15). 
  • Tuesday 15 December-  Christmas Party Chez Handel. Explore the whole of Handel’s House by candlelight, exquisitely adorned with festive decorations. With a mince pie and a glass in hand, immerse yourself in a richly atmospheric and elegant celebration while being serenaded by musicians and carol singers presenting pop-up performances of seasonal music inspired by the sounds Handel himself would have known. Tickets £55. 

The house is open Wednesday – Sunday 10.00 – 17.00 (last entry at 16:00). Tickets to 30 Novemberare £14.50 for adults, £10.50 for students and free for the under 12s.  From 1 December tickets are £16.00 for adults, £12.00 students, young person (12-17 years) £6.00, children 11 years and under go free.  Tickets available online or at the door.
