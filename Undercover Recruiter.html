Hiring a developer in Poland or a sales lead in Brazil used to mean setting up a local company first. An employer of record (EOR) removes that step: it becomes the legal employer in that country, runs payroll and benefits, and carries the compliance risk, while you manage the person’s day-to-day work. For US HR teams it is the fastest way to hire abroad without an entity, but providers differ a lot on price, country coverage and whether they actually own the entities that employ your people.

Quick answer: Deel is the best EOR for most companies, with 130+ countries and month-to-month terms. Remote is the pick if you want an EOR that only hires through entities it owns. Multiplier has the lowest fully published price among the big platforms, and Rippling makes sense if you already run HR and payroll on Rippling. Large enterprises should get a quote from G-P.

EOR vs PEO vs contractors: which do you need?

An EOR is for hiring full-time employees in countries where you have no legal entity. The EOR is the legal employer; you direct the work. A PEO is a US arrangement where you already employ people through your own entity and share employer duties with the PEO to get better benefits and outsourced HR admin. If your team is US-based, our guide to the best PEO services is the better starting point.

Contractors are the cheapest route, but only when the person genuinely works as an independent business. If you set their hours, give them equipment and treat them like staff, many countries will see them as employees, with back taxes and penalties to follow. Most EORs also sell contractor management for $29 to $49 a month, and some offer contractor of record, where the provider takes on the misclassification risk.

An EOR stops making financial sense once you have roughly 10 to 15 people in one country. At that point, setting up your own entity and running local payroll is usually cheaper, and every provider below can help with that move.

The best employer of record services in 2027

1. Deel

Deel is the biggest name in the category and the safest default for most companies. Its EOR covers 130+ countries, and the same platform handles contractors, global payroll for your own entities, a US PEO and immigration support.

Best for: Companies that want broad coverage, fast onboarding and the option to mix employees and contractors on one platform.

  • EOR in 130+ countries, with Deel saying it owns entities in more than 100 of them
  • Contractor management and contractor of record in the same account
  • Global payroll for countries where you have your own entity
  • Visa and immigration support, plus device and IT add-ons
  • Month-to-month terms with no long-term commitment

Pricing: EOR is $599 per employee per month. Contractor management is $49 per contractor per month, contractor of record is $325 per contractor per month, and the US PEO is $125 per employee per month.

Limitations: At $599 it is not the cheapest option, and costs add up quickly once you layer on benefits, devices and other add-ons. Support quality can vary by country because the platform covers so many markets.

Skip it if: You only need one or two hires in a single country and want the lowest monthly fee.

2. Remote

Remote sells itself on one difference: it employs people only through entities it owns, with no third-party partners in between. That gives you one company accountable for payroll, benefits and compliance in every country it covers.

Best for: Compliance-minded companies that want to know exactly who employs their people in each country.

  • EOR in 90+ countries, all through Remote-owned entities
  • Average onboarding of about three days, according to Remote
  • Contractor management from $29 and contractor of record from $325 per month
  • Global payroll from $19 per employee per month for your own entities
  • US PEO from $99 per employee per month

Pricing: EOR starts at $599 per employee per month on annual contracts. The Remote platform is included with no base fee.

Limitations: Owning every entity means coverage is narrower than Deel or G-P, at 90+ countries. The $599 rate applies to annual contracts, so check what month-to-month costs before you sign.

Skip it if: You need to hire in a country Remote doesn’t cover yet. Check its country list first.

3. Multiplier

Multiplier published its full EOR pricing in 2026, and it undercuts Deel and Remote by more than $100 a month per employee. It says it employs people through its own entities in most markets rather than renting partners’.

Best for: Cost-conscious companies that want a published, predictable price without a sales call.

  • EOR in 150+ countries, with owned entities in most of them
  • Core and Growth plans, plus custom Enterprise pricing
  • Contractor payments from $40 per contractor per month
  • Global payroll from $20 per person per month, billed annually
  • Payroll in 120+ currencies

Pricing: Core EOR is $459 per employee per month billed annually or $499 monthly. Growth is $519 annually or $559 monthly. Implementation fees and compliance-mandated add-ons are extra, and about 11% of countries have adjusted pricing.

Limitations: The headline rate excludes implementation fees, and some countries cost more than the list price. The brand is less established in the US than Deel or Remote.

Skip it if: You want one provider for a large US workforce as well as international hires.

4. Oyster

Oyster was built for distributed-first companies and is open about its fees. It charges nothing extra for setup, onboarding, offboarding or terminations, which many rivals bill separately.

Best for: Startups and remote-first teams that value transparent fees and good onboarding support.

  • EOR in 120+ countries, using local partners where Oyster has no entity
  • Contractor contracts in 180+ countries, free for 30 days and then $29 per contractor per month
  • No setup, onboarding, offboarding or termination fees
  • Startup discounts of up to $1,200 on EOR fees, plus programs for nonprofits and refugee hires
  • Salary benchmarking data across 130+ countries

Pricing: EOR is $699 per employee per month, with annual discounts available. A refundable deposit is required, and optional People Partner Services cost $300 per hour.

Limitations: At $699 it has the highest list price here, and some countries run through partners rather than Oyster’s own entities. You will need to fund a deposit before onboarding starts.

Skip it if: Price is your main deciding factor, or you need EOR coverage in more than 120 countries.

5. Papaya Global

Papaya Global started as a global payroll platform and is strongest for companies that run payroll through their own entities in some countries and use an EOR in others. Its payroll and payments tools are more developed than most EOR-first rivals’.

Best for: Mid-size and larger companies that need global payroll and EOR on one system.

  • Full-service payroll from $12 per employee per month and a platform license from $3
  • Contractor management from $2 per contractor per month
  • Payroll and payments in 160+ countries
  • No minimum number of workers
  • A 60-day opt-out clause in the standard agreement

Pricing: EOR is $650 per employee per month in most countries, or €650 in Europe. A refundable deposit applies.

Limitations: The EOR fee is higher than Deel or Remote, and much of the EOR is delivered through in-country partners. Payroll setup and year-end filing fees are extra.

Skip it if: You only need EOR and have no plans to run payroll through your own entities.

6. Rippling

Rippling is an HR, payroll and IT platform first, with EOR added on. If your US team already lives in Rippling, adding international hires to the same system is the main reason to choose it.

Best for: Companies already using Rippling for US HR and payroll.

  • EOR in 80+ countries through owned entities and local partners
  • Contractor payments in 185+ countries
  • One employee record for HR, payroll, devices and app access
  • Global payroll for your own entities
  • Works with the rest of the Rippling suite, including IT and spend

Pricing: Rippling doesn’t list EOR prices on its pricing page, so you will need a quote. A 2025 Rippling article put its EOR at $499 per employee per month, but treat that as a guide only.

Limitations: EOR coverage is narrower than the specialists, and pricing is less transparent. You will get the most value only if you also buy the wider Rippling platform. See our HR software guide for how Rippling compares as an HRIS.

Skip it if: You don’t use Rippling today and only need international hiring.

7. Pebl

Pebl is the new name for Velocity Global, one of the older EOR providers. It offers wide country coverage and a lower published starting price than most rivals, with an emphasis on compliance and local benefits.

Best for: Companies that want wide coverage and strong compliance support at a lower starting rate.

  • EOR in 185+ countries
  • Pebl says 90% of the employees it supports are on Pebl-owned entities
  • Compliance guidance vetted by law firm Baker McKenzie
  • Global benefits, pensions, equity and immigration services
  • Integrations and an API for your existing HR stack

Pricing: EOR starts at $399 per employee per month, but Pebl asks you to request a quote, so your final price may differ. Other services are quote-only.

Limitations: The $399 rate is a starting point rather than a fixed list price, and add-ons are priced separately. The rebrand from Velocity Global means fewer recent reviews under the Pebl name.

Skip it if: You want a fixed price you can see before speaking to sales.

8. G-P

G-P (Globalization Partners) created the EOR model in 2012 and remains a common choice for large enterprises. Its Meridian suite covers EOR, contractors and global hiring tools, with a large in-house legal and HR team behind it.

Best for: Large enterprises with complex compliance needs and high hiring volumes.

  • EOR in 180+ countries
  • Meridian Core and Meridian Prime EOR packages
  • G-P Contractor for independent workers
  • Salary benchmarking and hiring-cost data
  • Integrations with major HCM and payroll systems

Pricing: G-P does not publish prices. Quote on request.

Limitations: The lack of published pricing makes it hard to compare, and it is generally seen as a premium option. Smaller companies may find it more than they need.

Skip it if: You are a startup or small business hiring your first few people abroad.

What an EOR really costs

The monthly fee is the smallest part of the bill. On top of it you pay the employee’s gross salary, the employer’s share of taxes and social security (which runs above 30% of salary in some countries), mandatory benefits such as 13th-month pay, and any extra benefits you offer. Many providers also ask for a refundable deposit before your hire starts, so budget for that cash up front.

Before signing, ask each provider for a full cost estimate for your actual hire, and check these extras:

  • Setup, onboarding and termination fees
  • Currency conversion spreads when you fund payroll
  • Deposit size and how quickly it is refunded
  • Price differences for specific countries
  • Whether the provider owns the entity in your country or uses a partner

Compare the fee structure too. A flat fee per employee is easier to budget for than a percentage of salary, which gets expensive for senior hires.

How to choose an EOR

Start with your countries. List every country you plan to hire in over the next year and check each provider covers them through its own entity. Coverage headlines are not the same as owned entities.

Compare total cost, not the list price. Get quotes for the same hire from two or three providers, including taxes, benefits, deposits and FX.

Check benefits quality. In competitive markets, the private health insurance and pension options an EOR offers can decide whether a candidate accepts.

Look at the exit. Ask what it costs to terminate an employee and to move people onto your own entity later. Good providers make this straightforward.

Think about your stack. If you run HR on Rippling or payroll through your own entities, an EOR that plugs into that setup will save admin. Our payroll software guide covers US options.

Rules change often, so ask how each provider keeps contracts compliant. Singapore’s recent changes are a good example: see how Singapore’s EOR rules affect your workforce.

FAQ

What is an employer of record?

An employer of record is a company that legally employs workers on your behalf in a country where you have no entity. It handles contracts, payroll, taxes, benefits and compliance, while you manage the person’s daily work.

How much does an EOR cost?

Most EORs charge $400 to $700 per employee per month in service fees, and enterprise providers can charge more. That is on top of salary, employer taxes, statutory benefits and any deposit.

Is an EOR legal?

Yes, EORs are legal in most countries, though some restrict them. Germany, for example, limits how long a worker can be placed through an EOR, so check local rules for each country you hire in.

What’s the difference between an EOR and a PEO?

An EOR employs people for you in countries where you have no entity, while a PEO co-employs staff you already employ through your own US entity. EORs are for international hiring, and PEOs are mainly for US benefits and HR admin.

Can a UK or EU company use these EORs?

Yes, all eight providers serve companies based outside the US and can hire in the UK and EU. Papaya Global, for example, prices its EOR at €650 per employee per month in Europe.

When should you set up your own entity instead?

It usually pays to set up your own entity once you have about 10 to 15 employees in one country. Below that, an EOR is typically cheaper and faster.
