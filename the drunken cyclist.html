Having Dinner with Laurent-Perrier

Posted on November 18, 2026 by the drunken cyclist
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by

Late in the summer, when I actually found myself home for more than 24 hours, we hosted another wine dinner. This time the “guest” was Caroline Ricca, from the New York office of Laurent-Perrier Champagne. Why she chose to come to Houston in August is not entirely clear, but we were happy to have her and taste through a few of the domaine’s wines.

NV Laurent-Perrier Champagne Brut, Champagne, France: Retail $50-75. Extremely Heavy Bottle (834g; 29.4oz). Chardonnay +50%, Pinot Noir 30-35%, Meunier 10-15%. Dosage: 8-9 g/l. 4 years on lees. A Laurent-Perrier representative from NYC was in Houston, so we decided to have a little wine dinner at my house for a few wine writers and friends. We started with their flagship wine, La Cuvée, which I paired with a sesame-encrusted tuna and a wasabi, miso aioli. The pairing was pretty stellar as it seemed to elevate both the wine and the hors-d’œuvre. While the notes are similar to when I last tasted this in June (91 Points), I am bumping this up a notch. Excellent. 92 Points.

NV Laurent-Perrier Champagne Cuvée Rosé Brut, Champagne, France: Retail $90. Extremely heavy bottle (897g; 31.5oz). 100% Pinot Noir. Last night, we had a wine dinner at our house for the kind folks at Laurent-Perrier. Seeing that they wanted to focus on their iconic Cuvée Rosé, I decided on a menu with a decided Korean flair. We started with my wife’s classic hamul paejeon, a seafood pancake of sorts that worked quite nicely with this voluptuous and fruity macerated rosé. The wine really showed well, however, with my kimchi-bulgogi arancini and its gochujang-kewpie sauce. The fruit easily mitigated the spice, leaving the layered and complex flavors to be enjoyed on a basically fresh palate. When I tasted this a few months ago, I loved it (95 Points). My feelings have not waned. Outstanding. 95 Points.

NV Laurent-Perrier Champagne Héritage Brut, Champagne, France: Retail $115. Extremely Heavy Bottle (889g; 31.3oz). 55% Chardonnay, 45% Pinot Noir. 40 Crus of which 50% Grands Crus. When Laurent-Perrier came over for a wine dinner at our house, this Héritage was a bit of a surprise inclusion (at least for me). We had discussed a meal that was a bit out of the mainstream, which would showcase the versatility of champagne in general and Laurent-Perrier in particular. Once we decided on a Korean-themed dinner and the desire of Laurent-Perrier to highlight La Cuvée Rosé, I assumed the dinner would center around that wine and the prestige Cuvée Alexandra Rosé (more on that below). So this Héritage (a relatively new addition to the L-P “gamme”; a blend of only reserve wines) I saw as a bit of an outlier. But for me? This wine stole the show (OK, I knew the Alexandra would be incredible, so that was not really a surprise like this wine was). Initially, it was quite subtle, particularly when compared to the fruitier and more muscular rosés, but while the pink wines excelled in the juicy fruit department, this Heritage was oozing a tangy, bright acidity. That tartness served to wipe the palate of any lingering spice, paving the way for layer upon layer of complexity and depth. I expected the fruitier rosés to have that palate-cleansing effect (being married to a Korean-American, I have been down this wine-pairing-with-Korean-food path before), and yes, I knew that champagne is a spectacular match for all Asian food, but this truly was a surprise, how this wine not only held its own, but found a way to stand out among those myriad flavors. Similar notes to when I last tasted this wine. Outstanding. 96 Points.

2012 Laurent-Perrier Champagne Grande Cuvée Rosé Alexandra, Champagne, France: Retail $400. 80% Pinot Noir and 20% Chardonnay from 100% Grand Cru vineyards. Alexandra, first produced to celebrate its namesake’s wedding, is only produced when the Grand Cru Pinot Noir and Chardonnay are ripe at the same precise time, which, apparently, does not happen as often as one might think. This is my first interaction with the Cuvée, which was a delightful addition to a wine dinner that we hosted for Laurent-Perrier. I made a dinner with a Korean “flair”, highlighting the spicy, umami, and sweet flavors that I have come to love since marrying my Korean-American wife just over 25 years ago. One of the darker rosés on the market, for sure, nearly a light red in the glass, due to the extended maceration that L-P employs. Rich, even intense red fruit on the nose with a lovely Bing cherry predominant. The palate is initially all about the fruit, followed by a zingy exuberance despite its now 14 years in the bottle. Whoa. After that first wave of fruit, the wine slowly reveals its power and intensity, but this wine is both subtly decadent and possesses a laser-sharp acidity. Yes, this is amazing, or more, but it is also on the nervous side right now, perhaps overjoyed by being released from the bottle. In my mind, despite its already dozen-plus years of aging (ten on the lees) it is but a baby. Again, incredible right now, but in ten, twenty, thirty (?) years? This is the first wine in my lifetime that I can honestly say will outlive me. Outstanding Plus. 98 Points.
